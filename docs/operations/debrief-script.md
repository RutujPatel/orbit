# Shadow ORBIT Post-Review Debrief Script

## Purpose

Use this script immediately after each real review where practical.

The objective is to identify usefulness, misleading behavior, missing context, duplication, and reasons for non-use.

The debrief is not a sales conversation.

Do not defend the prototype.

Do not explain away criticism.

Do not ask only whether the manager liked it.

## Opening

Say:

> Thank you. I want to separate whether the system worked as designed from whether it actually helped you. I am going to ask a few short questions about this specific review.

## Core questions

Ask these questions in this order.

### 1. Removal

> If you had to remove something from the review we just used, what would you remove first?

Follow-up only if needed:

> What made that unnecessary, distracting, or repetitive?

### 2. Usefulness

> What, if anything, was genuinely useful in this review?

Follow-up only if needed:

> Did it save preparation, change the discussion, confirm something important, or help with follow-through?

Do not supply those examples unless the manager struggles to interpret the question.

### 3. Misleading output

> What was misleading, wrong, or too confident?

Follow-ups:

> Was the source data wrong, was the rule wrong, or was the explanation wrong?

> Did anything look precise but fail to match how your team actually works?

### 4. Missing context

> What important thing did Shadow ORBIT miss?

Follow-ups:

> Where did that information actually live?

> Could it reasonably have been in Jira, or does it belong somewhere else?

> Would you want a product to automate that context, or would manual context be sufficient?

### 5. Existing knowledge

> Which of the findings or changes did you already know before seeing Shadow ORBIT?

Follow-up:

> Did seeing them here still help, or was it only repetition?

### 6. New information

> Did Shadow ORBIT show you anything you did not already know?

Follow-ups:

> Did that change what you discussed or decided?

> Would you have discovered it through your normal preparation anyway?

### 7. Evidence and trust

> When you opened evidence, what were you trying to verify?

If no evidence was opened:

> Did you trust the explanation without opening the evidence, or did the evidence simply not seem worth checking?

Follow-up:

> Would a direct Jira link have been enough?

### 8. Decisions

> Did this review produce decisions that should be remembered later?

Follow-ups:

> Were those decisions actually made here, or had they already been made elsewhere?

> Where would you normally record them?

### 9. Commitments

> Did recording commitments here feel useful or duplicative?

Follow-ups:

> Where would each commitment normally live?

> Which place would you treat as authoritative?

> Who would be responsible for keeping the two places consistent?

### 10. Carry-forward

For week two and later:

> Did seeing the prior commitment or prior finding context help, or did it feel like repetitive alerting?

Follow-up:

> What would have made the repeated item more useful or less annoying?

### 11. Work introduced

> What extra work did Shadow ORBIT create for you or the team?

Follow-up:

> Was that work only because the prototype is new, or would it continue every week?

### 12. Continued use

> Would you choose to use this again for next week's review?

If yes:

> Which part would you be disappointed to lose?

If no or uncertain:

> What is the main reason you would return to the current process?

### 13. Removal test

> If Shadow ORBIT were unavailable next week, what would you do differently?

This question tests dependency more strongly than stated enthusiasm.

### 14. Stop-using condition

> What would make you stop using a product like this?

Do not challenge the answer.

## Week-four commercial questions

Ask only after repeated use has occurred.

### Budget relevance

> Is this useful enough that your organization would pay to continue using it?

### Buyer

> Who would own that budget?

### Approval

> Who would need to approve the product, Jira access, security review, and contract?

### Concrete continuation

> What concrete next step would have to happen for you to continue after this experiment?

Do not treat a positive hypothetical answer as equivalent to a paid commitment.

## Closing

Say:

> Thank you. We will treat what happened in this review as evidence, and we will not assume that technical correctness means the workflow is valuable.

## Interviewer discipline

- Capture exact language where practical.
- Separate observed behavior from interpretation.
- Record silence, hesitation, and non-use.
- Do not convert feature requests directly into roadmap items.
- Do not imply that missing integrations are already planned.
- Do not promise that every source-data problem will be solved.
- Log any resulting experiment change in the system-change log.
